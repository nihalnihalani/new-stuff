## The one that matters most
**From December, ChatGPT Plus, Pro and Business will share one token budget across ChatGPT and Codex.** This comes as OpenAI folds its agentic "Work" mode into the main ChatGPT interface. Heavier models and longer tasks use up the budget faster. When it runs out you wait for a reset, upgrade, or buy credits. Chats with the Instant models won't count. If you use Codex on a Plus plan for hackathon builds, your coding and your everyday chatting will now come out of the same allowance, so plan big agent runs around your reset times. The Decoder calls it the end of "all-you-can-prompt" pricing. [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openais-upcoming-chat-and-work-merger-in-chatgpt-signals-the-end-of-all-you-can-prompt-pricing/) · 15:21, 1 Nov

## AI news

### OpenAI's products and pricing
- OpenAI is adding device and payment-card limits to paid ChatGPT plans (Go, Plus and the Pro tiers). If too many subscriptions share one card or device, you can't start a new one, and you'll see "Device limit reached". Logging out doesn't free up a slot. This ends the trick of rotating several cheap accounts on one laptop to get more usage. OpenAI points people to buying credits instead, which usually costs more. [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-is-steering-chatgpt-users-toward-more-paid-usage/) · 17:35, 1 Nov

### Research worth knowing
- Adobe Research and UC San Diego built EditAgent, an image-editing agent that picks a specialist tool for each step, like sharpening or zooming. It then has an ordinary language model compare the results using measured statistics and close-up crops. In a test of 600 edits, the general editor Flux-2-Klein-9B failed 42.7% of the time on its own. EditAgent does better but needs about 10 times the compute. The pattern of "specialist tools plus a judge that checks numbers" carries over to any agent you build. [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/adobe-is-testing-how-well-ai-agents-can-use-editing-tools-like-a-photoshop-professional-would/) · 20:26, 1 Nov
- A team led by Google built StreamVibe, a video assistant that watches you cook or assemble furniture and decides for itself when to speak up. Its benchmark has about 4,100 examples from 183+ hours of recordings, and the paper shows how hard that "when should I help?" judgement still is for today's models. It's a good problem to pick for a vision-agent hackathon project. [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-wants-video-ai-to-understand-what-people-need-as-they-workgoogle-researchers-are-teaching-a-video-assistant-when-to-offer-help/) · 16:55, 1 Nov
- Vals AI tested Chinese, American and other models on hundreds of questions about China. Chinese models leaned toward Beijing's positions less than expected, and GLM-5.3 Flash said plainly that China's human-rights abuses are well documented. Prompting in Simplified Chinese made most models more pro-government, and some Western models sometimes took the official line too. Check this if you're choosing a cheap open model for a product with political or news content. [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/chinese-ai-models-are-politically-complex-not-just-government-mouthpieces-study-finds/) · 18:03, 1 Nov

## Benchmarks and leaderboards
- **Which JavaScript framework is cheapest for an agent to build with?** Nolan Lawson had Opus 5.5 build the same Pokémon app 5 times in each of 12 frameworks, then checked each build against a test suite and Lighthouse. The median cost per build ranged from $2.67 to $4.97. SvelteKit, Gracile+Lit and Angular were reliably the most expensive, partly because the agent kept reading SvelteKit's new migration guide. Agents also often ignored the frameworks' official MCPs and skills. 315 points on HN. [post](https://nolanlawson.com/2026/10/28/the-ai-cost-of-javascript-frameworks/) · [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50323088) · 11:36, 1 Nov

## Build idea of the day
Enter **Build Your First AI App** (students only, closes 28 Nov) with an offline exam-prep assistant. Photograph old question papers or handwritten notes, and **Xiaomi-OCR-0** (0.8B, Apache-2.0) turns them into structured JSON questions and answers. You ask questions out loud through **Handy**'s offline speech-to-text, and a local model served by **mlx-serve** on a Mac answers through its OpenAI-compatible API, so it costs nothing to run. Run **iFixAi** on it so you can show judges the assistant does what it's supposed to.
